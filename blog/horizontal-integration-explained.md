<h1>Horizontal Integration Explained: Definition, Exam</h1>
<p><strong>Horizontal integration</strong> is a growth strategy where a company expands its operations at the same stage of the production or distribution chain—usually by acquiring or merging with competitors.</p>

<div class="callout">
<p>🔑 <strong>Key takeaway:</strong> Horizontal integration lets firms increase market share, achieve economies of scale, and reduce competition.</p>
</div>

<h2>What Is Horizontal Integration?</h2>
<p>Imagine a smartphone manufacturer that only makes phones. If it buys another phone maker, it is expanding horizontally—adding more of the same product line. This differs from <em>vertical integration</em>, where a company moves up or down the supply chain (e.g., a retailer acquiring a logistics firm).</p>

<h2>Why Companies Pursue Horizontal Integration</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Market Share Boost</strong> – Combine customer bases to dominate a market segment.</li>
<li><strong>Cost Synergies</strong> – Consolidate production, R&amp;D, and marketing to lower per‑unit costs.</li>
<li><strong>Competitive Advantage</strong> – Remove rivals, increase pricing power.</li>
<li><strong>Revenue Diversification</strong> – Add new product lines that are closely related.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Famous Real‑World Examples</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Disney + 21st Century Fox (2019)</strong> – Expanded Disney’s film library and streaming content.</li>
<li><strong>Facebook + Instagram (2012)</strong> – Consolidated social‑media advertising platforms.</li>
<li><strong>Exxon + Mobil (1999)</strong> – Created the world’s largest oil refiner.</li>
<li><strong>Heinz + Kraft (2015)</strong> – Combined two food‑processing giants to cut costs.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to Answer the Exam Question</h2>
<p>Most finance or business‑strategy exams ask you to <em>define horizontal integration</em> and then <em>evaluate its pros and cons</em>. Follow this three‑part structure:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Definition (1‑2 sentences)</strong> – State the core idea and contrast with vertical integration.</li>
<li><strong>Benefits (3‑4 bullet points)</strong> – Cite market‑share, cost synergies, competitive advantage, and revenue diversification.</li>
<li><strong>Risks/Drawbacks (2‑3 bullet points)</strong> – Mention antitrust concerns, integration costs, cultural clashes, and over‑concentration risk.</li>
</ol>
<p>Finish with a brief example (e.g., Disney‑Fox) to illustrate the concept in practice.</p>
